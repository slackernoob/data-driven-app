A quick guide

Each row is one hour of your day ahead. The bar shows that hour's WBGT — the shorter the bar, the easier the run. The highlighted rows are your window.

WBGT isn't air temperature. It rolls heat, humidity, sun and wind into one number for how hard your body has to work to stay cool. Humidity is what makes it bite here: 30° WBGT is much tougher going than 30° on a thermometer, because your sweat can't evaporate and the heat has nowhere to go.

Struck-out hours have rain on the way. Anything with a chance or higher is set aside, however kind the heat looks. On a thundery day that can rule out most of the afternoon — that's Singapore, not a glitch.

A note on the numbers: this WBGT is estimated from Open-Meteo's hourly forecast rather than measured, and the way it's derived makes it read a little low — so treat borderline hours as slightly worse than shown. It isn't NEA's official WBGT, and the cut-offs assume you're already used to the heat. It's best for choosing between hours rather than for judging whether conditions are genuinely dangerous.
